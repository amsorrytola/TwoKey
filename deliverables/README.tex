\section{Introduction}

Enterprise AI has stopped answering questions and started taking actions. An insurance
claims agent now reads a file, assesses the damage and settles the claim, with money
leaving the business in minutes and no human in the loop. Every mainstream AI safety
tool inspects the text a model produces. The financial and regulatory damage, however,
does not occur in the text. It occurs at the moment of execution, at the tool call, and
that layer is currently ungoverned.

Interlock is a runtime assurance layer for agentic AI. It applies the Simplex pattern
used in avionics certification, consisting of an untrusted controller, a verified safety
monitor and a safe fallback. Before an agent can act it must declare a schema-pinned plan
called an intent contract. Every action then passes six independent verification checks
running in parallel, which are fused into a four-axis risk vector and routed into one of
five graduated autonomy lanes. Every decision is written to a hash-chained,
cryptographically signed ledger, and human overrides feed back into recalibration of the
system's own thresholds.

The prototype is a functioning system rather than a mock-up. It runs real language models
from two independent vendors, executes genuine parallel verification, writes a genuine
tamper-evident audit trail, and reports measured precision, recall, false positive and
false negative rates against seeded ground truth.

\subsection{Why the action layer is different}

\vspace{2pt}
{\small
\begin{xltabular}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.20\textwidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
\thead{Property} & \thead{Consequence} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Irreversibility} & A wrong answer is corrected with a follow-up message. A wrong settlement is money that has already left the account, and a wrong denial is a regulatory event. \\
\textbf{Compounding} & Agents call other agents. A single questionable action propagates into downstream systems before any person reviews it. \\
\textbf{Invisibility} & Output monitoring reports that the model behaved normally. The claim text was fluent, the tone was appropriate, and the payout was still wrong. \\
\bottomrule
\end{xltabular}}

Insurers already lose an estimated five to ten percent of claim payouts to leakage.
Autonomy amplifies that exposure at machine speed. A Canadian tribunal has held an
airline liable for a commitment made by its chatbot, establishing that an organisation
owns what its AI says. An agent that executes converts an apology into a payout.

\subsection{Regulatory position}

The EU AI Act classifies AI used in insurance risk assessment and pricing as high risk.
Article 12 requires automatic logging of events over the system lifetime, retained by
deployers for a minimum of six months. Article 14 requires that high-risk systems be
designed so that natural persons can effectively oversee them. In India, the Digital
Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025 impose consent, purpose
limitation and data minimisation obligations that apply directly to AI deployment, and
IRDAI has clarified that attributing a discriminatory outcome to an algorithm is not a
defence. These obligations differ by jurisdiction and continue to evolve, which is why
they are expressed as configuration rather than as code.

\section{Architecture}

\begin{callout}
\kicker{design principle}
The language model never decides. Language models propose actions and assess evidence.
The lane is selected by deterministic thresholds read from a YAML policy file, and money
moves only through an idempotent executor protected by a budget breaker. This separation
is auditable and is reported explicitly in the Trust Report screen.
\end{callout}

\subsection{The intent contract}

The agent cannot reach a tool directly. It can only emit a Pydantic-validated contract
stating the action it intends, the parameters, a numbered rationale, the policy clauses
it relied upon and its own confidence. Everything downstream verifies this structured
object rather than free prose. This single constraint is what makes the rest of the
system possible.

\subsection{The verification mesh}

Six checks run concurrently through \code{asyncio}. The methods are deliberately
heterogeneous, because correlated detectors fail together.

\vspace{2pt}
{\small
\begin{xltabular}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.175\textwidth}L{0.215\textwidth}L{0.075\textwidth}R{0.115\textwidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
\thead{Check} & \thead{Method} & \thead{LLM} & \thead{Latency} & \thead{What it catches} \\
\midrule
\code{static\_policy} & Ten deterministic rules over the governed database & \textcolor{ok}{No} & 2 to 5 ms & Over-limit payouts, duplicates, missing entities, PII in the payload, late filing \\
\code{consequence\_sim} & Dry run against a sandboxed copy of the database & \textcolor{ok}{No} & 1 to 5 ms & Blast radius, irreversibility, budget breach \\
\code{prompt\_guard} & Llama Prompt Guard 2 classifier & Small & 130 ms & Prompt injection embedded in claim text \\
\code{evidence\_nli} & Entailment judge over the cited clauses & Yes & 0.4 to 1.3 s & Decisions that contradict the policy they cite \\
\code{trace\_auditor} & Per-step process reward over the rationale & Yes & 0.6 to 1.4 s & Reasoning steps asserting unsupported facts \\
\code{semantic\_entropy} & k resamples clustered by meaning, then entropy & Statistical & 1.2 to 6.4 s & Hallucination and genuine model uncertainty \\
\bottomrule
\end{xltabular}}

\subsection{Risk fusion}

The Round 2 brief observes that bias, hallucination and privacy risks overlap in
practice. A single scalar conceals this, so Interlock emits a four-axis vector alongside
the policy-weighted scalar used for routing.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hallucination.} Derived from evidence entailment, semantic entropy and trace validity.
\item \textbf{Privacy.} Derived from PII detected in the outbound payload and injection pressure.
\item \textbf{Bias.} Derived from the cohort disparity signal reported by the drift sentinel.
\item \textbf{Blast radius.} Derived from money moved, reversibility, systems touched and downstream fan-out.
\end{itemize}

Two overrides sit above the weighted average. A deterministic hard failure, such as a
policy or customer record that does not exist, cannot be averaged away by soft checks
passing. Evidence drawn only from low-trust sources adds a proportional penalty.

\subsection{Graduated autonomy}

\vspace{2pt}
{\small
\begin{xltabular}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.13\textwidth}L{0.05\textwidth}YR{0.12\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\thead{Lane} & \thead{Mark} & \thead{Behaviour} & \thead{Observed} \\
\midrule
\lane{ok}{AUTO} & \lane{ok}{>} & Executes immediately beneath the conformal error ceiling & 13.6\% \\
\lane{warn}{EDIT} & \lane{warn}{/} & The action is mechanically repaired and then executed. Payouts are clamped to the coverage limit, PII is redacted, and two-key amount disagreements are reconciled downward & see below \\
\lane{acnpurple}{TWO-KEY} & \lane{acnpurple}{||} & A model from a second vendor, on a separate credential, must independently reach the same decision & 40.9\% \\
\lane{warn}{HUMAN} & \lane{warn}{\^{}} & Staged for a reviewer with the evidence already prepared & 31.8\% \\
\lane{bad}{BLOCK} & \lane{bad}{X} & Fails closed. Phantom entities, prompt injections and duplicate settlements never execute & 13.6\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{xltabular}}

The EDIT lane is significant. Most real failures are not category errors requiring a full
stop, they are actions that are nearly correct. Blocking those trains staff to ignore the
system, which is precisely the alert fatigue the brief warns against. Introducing
conservative reconciliation in this lane reduced the false positive rate from 41.2 percent
to 14.3 percent with no change in the false negative count.

\subsection{Two-Key concurrence}

High-consequence or over-cap actions require two independent keys to turn. Key one is
\code{openai/gpt-oss-120b} served by Groq, supported by Qwen judges. Key two is
\code{deepseek-v4-flash} served by DeepSeek, a different vendor on different
infrastructure under a separate credential. A faulty deployment, a poisoned fine-tune, a
leaked key or a correlated blind spot within one model family therefore cannot turn both
keys.

If the two keys disagree on the action, the item escalates to a human. If they agree on
the action but differ on the amount, Interlock settles at the more conservative figure
and records the disagreement, which is safer than escalating and safer than accepting the
higher number.

\subsection{Safe execution and the ledger}

The executor applies idempotency keys derived from the claim, action and amount, so a
repeated instruction cannot produce a second payment. Each action expands into a saga of
four compensable stages, so a downstream failure unwinds the money movement rather than
leaving the world half changed. A daily budget breaker caps aggregate exposure per
currency.

Every verdict, override, policy change and recalibration is appended to a JSONL ledger.
Each entry carries the hash of its predecessor and an Ed25519 signature over its own hash.
Modifying any historical record invalidates every subsequent hash, and the verifier
reports the exact sequence number at which the chain broke. This satisfies Article 12 in a
form that can be demonstrated rather than asserted, and the demonstration is available as
a button in the interface.

\subsection{The learning loop}

Labelled verdicts, whether from human review or from evaluation, feed a split-conformal
calibration procedure. The business sets alpha, the error rate it is willing to accept on
unattended decisions. Tau, the routing threshold, is then derived as the finite-sample
corrected quantile of the risk scores of correct decisions, capped below the lowest
scoring known-incorrect decision. No threshold is chosen by hand. A drift sentinel reports
the unattended approval rate by customer cohort and opens a fairness review when the gap
exceeds fifteen percentage points.

\section{Recommended tooling}

None of the following is required to run the prototype. Each is recommended for a
production deployment and appears on the roadmap.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Open Policy Agent.} Compiles the policy packs to Rego with SMT checking, so policy conflicts are caught before deployment rather than at runtime.
\item \textbf{A third judge vendor.} Two of the three language model checks currently share the Qwen family. A third vendor removes the residual correlation.
\item \textbf{OpenTelemetry.} The engine already emits per-check timings; exporting them gives production tracing without further instrumentation.
\item \textbf{Accenture Trusted Agent Huddle.} Certifies and scores agents on admission. Interlock governs every action thereafter, so the two are complementary.
\end{itemize}

\section{Configuration}

All governance lives in \code{policies/*.yaml}. No threshold is held in code. Three packs
describe three concurrent AI use cases, each with its own risk appetite, latency budget
and check weighting.

\vspace{2pt}
{\small
\begin{xltabular}{\textwidth}{@{}YR{0.10\textwidth}R{0.16\textwidth}R{0.14\textwidth}R{0.16\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\thead{Use case} & \thead{Alpha} & \thead{Latency budget} & \thead{Auto ceiling} & \thead{Unattended cap} \\
\midrule
\code{claims-settlement} & 0.02 & 3,000 ms & 0.20 & EUR 2,500 \\
\code{customer-support} & 0.10 & 1,200 ms & 0.45 & EUR 200 \\
\code{internal-copilot} & 0.15 & 5,000 ms & 0.55 & not applicable \\
\bottomrule
\end{xltabular}}

Each pack carries jurisdiction overrides. Under the EU pack a claim denial requires a
human signature, reflecting Article 14 of the AI Act and Article 22 of the GDPR, and logs
are retained for six months. Under the India pack the same denial may proceed
automatically, PII handling follows DPDP rules and logs are retained for twelve months.

The prototype demonstrates this directly. Claim \code{CLM-2045} produces a risk score of
0.08 under both packs, and is routed to \lane{warn}{HUMAN} under the EU configuration and
\lane{ok}{AUTO} under the India configuration. No code changes between the two runs.

Policy can also be edited live from the console. The Autonomy Dial on the action stream
switches use case and jurisdiction, and every change is written to the ledger with its
author.

\subsection{Latency, stated honestly}

The Round 1 concept quoted a mesh latency of approximately twelve milliseconds. That
figure holds for the deterministic checks and is not accurate for the mesh as a whole once
language model judges are included. The measured profile is as follows.

\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\scriptsize, xleftmargin=6pt]
fast path (deterministic)   static_policy 2-5 ms      consequence_sim 1-5 ms
classifier                  prompt_guard 130 ms
slow path (LLM judges)      evidence_nli 0.4-1.3 s    trace_auditor 0.6-1.4 s
                            semantic_entropy 1.2-6.4 s
mesh wall clock             p50 6.5 s, against a serial sum 1.6 to 2.5x higher
\end{Verbatim}

Two engineering responses were made rather than concealing the figure. First, the checks
execute concurrently, and the interface displays the measured serial-to-parallel saving
for each individual action. Second, the Groq free tier meters eight thousand tokens per
minute per credential, so a token-aware key pool was built that reads the rate limit
headers and schedules across both credentials. That change alone reduced p50 from 10.6
seconds to 4.3 seconds.

\section{Troubleshooting and FAQ}

\subsubsection{The console shows offline and no actions appear.}
The verification service is not running or is on a different port. Start it with
\code{make api} and confirm \code{http://localhost:8000/api/state} responds. The console
reads its API base from \code{ui/.env.local}.

\subsubsection{Actions are slow, or a batch appears to stall.}
The Groq free tier meters eight thousand tokens per minute per credential. The key pool
throttles rather than failing, so throughput is bounded rather than broken. Provide both
Groq keys, or reduce batch concurrency.

\subsubsection{A check reports that it is unavailable.}
An unreachable judge returns a fail-safe risk score rather than a pass. Degradation
therefore increases escalation instead of permitting unverified execution. Check
credentials in \code{.env}.

\subsubsection{Recalibration refuses to run.}
It requires a minimum of five labelled verdicts. Run \code{make eval} to seed labels, or
clear items from the review queue.

\subsubsection{The ledger reports a breach.}
Either the tamper demonstration was triggered from the Trust Report, or a stored entry was
edited. Run \code{make reset} to rebuild the chain.

\subsubsection{Why is a correct denial still sent to a human?}
Under the EU pack an adverse automated decision requires a human signature. This is a
mandated review rather than a detection, and the evaluation counts it separately so that
it does not distort the false positive rate.

\subsubsection{Can this govern something other than insurance?}
Nothing in the architecture is domain specific. The same mesh governs a support assistant
and an internal copilot in the prototype, driven only by different policy packs.
